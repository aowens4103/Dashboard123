# Starting an RIA: Do You Need a B/D? And B/Ds Open to Outside RIAs

*Research date: September 23, 2026. Firm economics change often and most B/Ds do not publish grids or minimums. Verify every number in writing (ask for the rep agreement and fee schedule) before you sign.*

---

## 1. Short answer: do you need a B/D?

**No. You only need a B/D if you want to keep doing broker-dealer business.** A standalone RIA (your existing LLC registered with the state, or the SEC once you pass about $100M+ AUM) can run fee-based business with only a Series 65, or a 66 plus a 7. The RIA is not an "outside business activity" (OBA) of anything unless you also stay registered with a FINRA member firm.

You need a B/D relationship (a "hybrid" or "dually registered" setup) **only if at least one of these applies**:

| Reason | Why it requires a B/D |
|---|---|
| You want to keep **commission trails** (12b-1s on mutual funds, variable annuity trails) | Trails can only be paid to a registered rep through a B/D. If you drop the B/D, those trails stop or go to the B/D's house. |
| You want to sell **commissionable securities** (variable annuities, A-share funds, 529 A-shares, UITs, alternatives/REITs/BDCs, variable life) | These are securities sales, so they require FINRA registration. |
| You want to keep your **Series 6/7 active** long term | Licenses lapse 2 years after a U5, or up to 5 years if you enroll in FINRA's MQP (see §4). |
| Some clients need **brokerage accounts** that can't reasonably be moved to advisory | Accounts that are "reverse churning" risks, or buy-and-hold positions where an advisory fee is hard to justify. |

**What doesn't require a B/D:**
- Fee-based advisory and planning through your RIA, with custody at Schwab, Fidelity, Altruist, Pershing Advisor Solutions, LPL RIA custody, and so on.
- **Fixed and fixed-indexed annuities and life, LTC, and DI insurance.** These need only an insurance license and a BGA/IMO appointment, not a B/D.
- Advisory ("I-share" / no-commission) variable annuities sold through your RIA. These are available through the major RIA custodians' insurance platforms.

### If you *do* stay with a B/D: the OBA question
Yes. If you stay registered with a B/D, your RIA must be disclosed to and approved by the B/D as an outside activity. What the B/D must do with it is changing right now:

- **Current rules (still in force today):** FINRA Rule 3270 (OBAs) plus Rule 3280 (private securities transactions). Under 1990s guidance (NTM 94-44 / 96-33), advisory transactions a rep does at an outside RIA "for compensation" are treated as private securities transactions that the **B/D must supervise and record**. This is the main reason many B/Ds either forbid outside RIAs, take an **override** (a cut of your advisory revenue), or charge **platform/oversight fees** on outside RIA assets. Kitces reports these are typically **10–20 bps**.
- **New FINRA Rule 3290 (approved by the SEC on Sept 15, 2026; effective date not yet set):** This rule replaces 3270 and 3280. Activity at an SEC- or state-registered investment adviser is treated as an **outside activity, not an outside securities transaction**. It **eliminates the B/D's supervision and recordkeeping obligation for advisory activity at an unaffiliated RIA.** You still give the B/D notice, and the B/D still does a risk assessment and can impose conditions or prohibit the activity.
  - **What it means for you:** Once 3290 takes effect, B/Ds lose their main justification for overrides and oversight fees on your RIA's assets. Negotiate with that in mind. Ask each firm directly: *"What changes in your fees on my outside RIA when 3290 goes live?"* Until FINRA announces the effective date, the old 94-44 regime still applies.

## 2. Two kinds of B/D for a hybrid with its own RIA

| | "RIA-friendly" / "friendly" B/D (specialists) | Large independent B/D (IBD) with a hybrid program |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | PKS, Mutual Securities/Mutual Group, M.S. Howells, Private Client Services, Geneos, Arete | LPL, Cetera, Osaic, Kestra, Cambridge, Raymond James, Valmark |
| Override on your RIA fees | Usually **none** | Sometimes. More often a platform or admin fee on RIA assets they custody or oversee. |
| Commission payout | Typically ~85–92% (Mutual Group publishes 85–92%) | Grid based, often ~80–95%. LPL says 80–100% independent. |
| Production minimums | Low or none, but usually a minimum annual fee | More likely to have minimums or a small-producer surcharge. Some prefer you on their corporate RIA. |
| Best fit | Solo or small RIA whose B/D book is shrinking or legacy (trails + occasional VA) | You want a full tech/product shelf, an OSJ, or transition money, or your B/D book is still large |

## 4. Alternatives to a B/D

1. **Drop the B/D and enroll in FINRA's Maintaining Qualifications Program (MQP).** It keeps your Series 6/7 (and 24, etc.) valid for **up to 5 years** after the U5 instead of 2. The cost is **$100/year** plus annual CE, and you must enroll within 2 years of termination. You can't do securities business while in MQP, but you can re-affiliate later without retesting.
2. **Insurance-only via a BGA/IMO.** Fixed and indexed annuities and life/LTC/DI pay commissions under your insurance license, with no B/D required. Your RIA's ADV must disclose the conflict.
3. **Move commission assets to advisory**, or to advisory/I-share VAs, at your RIA custodian. Check suitability for each client, since a fee on buy-and-hold assets may not be in their best interest.
4. **Sell or transfer the trail book.** For example, PKS will buy or maintain commission books for reps going fee-only.

**Rule of thumb:** If your remaining B/D revenue (trails + new commissions) is less than about **$15–25k/year**, the costs usually eat most of it. Those costs are B/D annual fees, FINRA/state registrations, E&O, compliance time, and dual supervision of advertising and correspondence. Fee-only + MQP is often cleaner. Above that level, a friendly B/D with no override is usually worth it.
*(The threshold is a rough estimate from typical friendly-B/D cost structures, not a published figure. Run your own numbers with real quotes.)*

## 5. Questions to send every B/D (to get the unpublished numbers)

1. **Payout grid** on commissions (by product type and trails) and any **production minimum** or small-producer surcharge.
2. **Override or platform fee on my outside RIA's assets** (bps), and whether it drops when **FINRA Rule 3290** takes effect.
3. **Fixed annual costs:** B/D membership/affiliation fee, technology fee, compliance fee, **E&O** (is it required through them?), and FINRA/state registration pass-throughs.
4. **Ticket charges** and account fees (IRA custodial, inactivity, transfer-out). Also sweep/cash revenue sharing.
5. **Custody:** Can my RIA stay at my chosen custodian (Schwab, Fidelity, Altruist, Pershing)? Do they require any RIA assets on their platform?
6. **Supervision of my RIA:** today (94-44) and after 3290. Do they need access to my RIA's books, trade blotter, or billing?
7. **Advertising/communications review:** Will they review my RIA's website, social, and newsletters, and at what turnaround and cost?
8. **Trail transfer:** Can my existing trails be re-papered or "broker-of-record" changed to them? What's the timeline?
9. **Transition assistance** (forgivable loans), and whether it's tied to their corporate RIA (e.g., Cambridge).
10. **Exit terms:** notice period, and what happens to trails and accounts if I leave.

## 6. Suggested path (general info, not legal advice)

1. List your current B/D revenue by source (trails vs. new commissions) and which products truly need a B/D.
2. If it's small or declining: go **fee-only + MQP**, add a BGA for fixed/indexed annuities and insurance, and re-evaluate within 5 years.
3. If it's meaningful: get written quotes from **2–3 friendly B/Ds** (PKS, Mutual Group, M.S. Howells, PCS, Geneos). Add **LPL or Cetera** if you want a single custody + B/D platform.
4. Time the move against **Rule 3290's effective date**. Once it's effective, outside-RIA supervision obligations (and the fees built on them) go away.
5. Have securities counsel or a compliance consultant review the rep agreement and your ADV Part 2A conflict disclosures.
